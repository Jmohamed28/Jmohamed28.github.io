Clipper

A desktop tool that turns long videos into short vertical clips — and uploads them to its operator's own TikTok account.

What it does

  1. Takes a long video the operator has the right to use: a stream recording, a podcast, or footage supplied by a brand campaign.
  2. Transcribes it locally and scores it to find the moments worth cutting.
  3. Renders each moment as a 1080×1920 vertical clip with burned-in captions, framed on whoever is speaking.
  4. Puts the best clips in a queue, with a caption for each one.
  5. Uploads them to the operator's own TikTok account through TikTok's Content Posting API, on a schedule the operator sets.

Single operator, single account. Clipper runs on one person's computer and posts to that same person's TikTok account, connected through TikTok's own authorization screen. It is not a hosted service and has no other users.

How it connects to TikTok

The operator creates a TikTok developer app, authorizes it against their own account, and Clipper stores the resulting token locally. Uploads then go through the official Content Posting API. Clipper never automates the TikTok website or app, and never asks for a TikTok password — authorization happens entirely on TikTok's own pages.

Built on

  • Python, run from the command line on macOS
  • yt-dlp to fetch source video the operator links to
  • Whisper, running locally, for transcription and captions
  • ffmpeg and OpenCV for framing and rendering
  • TikTok's Content Posting API for uploading

Content rights

Clipper is used only on footage its operator is entitled to publish — their own recordings, or material licensed to them by a brand campaign. Where a clip is a paid partnership, it is declared as such in TikTok's commercial content settings and labelled in the caption.
